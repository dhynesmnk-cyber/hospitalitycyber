Three kinds of business share the word hospitality and almost none of their software. A hotel runs on a property management system, a cafe or pub on a point-of-sale system, and a bottle shop on retail point-of-sale and stock software. Three rules changed in 2026 for all of them. From 1 October the RBA removed surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa.[^1] From 1 July award rates rose 4.75 per cent.[^2] And from 1 July super must reach funds within 7 business days of payday.[^3]

This chapter maps the stacks and points to the chapters that cover each rule. Treat vendor names as examples, not endorsements.

## The three stacks

The table is my description of how the businesses differ. It is general knowledge, not a sourced finding, and the vendors are examples I have named across this Resource.

| | Accommodation | Venues (cafe, restaurant, bar, pub) | Retail food, wine and bev |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core system | Property management system (Opera Cloud, Mews, Cloudbeds, RMS Cloud, Little Hotelier) | POS (Lightspeed, Square, Zeller, Tyro, TouchBistro, OrderMate) | Retail POS and stock (Lightspeed Retail, Shopify, Square) |
| What you sell | A room-night, which cannot be stored | A seat and a plate | A unit of product |
| Data you hold | Guest identity, stay history, payment details | Names, numbers, dietary notes, spend | Loyalty, purchase history, age checks |

## Where they overlap

Payments, rostering, accounting and a Google Business Profile are common to all three. The surcharge ban reaches the terminal in every one of them (chapter 8). Pay and super rules apply to all three, though which award covers which staff depends on the kind of business (chapter 9). Chapter 11 covers guest data, and chapter 17 covers what the contracts say.

## Hybrids

A hotel with a restaurant, a winery with a cellar door and rooms, or a pub with a bottle shop needs two systems to agree. That means the property system talking to the POS for room charges and guest profiles, or two POS systems feeding one set of books.

My read: most failures in that case are integration failures, not feature gaps. The pay rules also differ with the kind of business: a restaurant inside a hotel and a café on its own premises raise a different award question (chapter 9). Ask each vendor for a named integration, a price for it, and a reference customer running it.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to each vendor in a hybrid venue.

1. Which named integration connects your product to our other system, and what does it cost per month?

2. Which customer of our type runs that integration today, and can we speak to them?

3. When either side changes its product or is sold, who is responsible for keeping the integration working?

4. What happens to our data in each system if we leave one?

## What this chapter does not show

- Market shares, distribution channels, commission rates or the share of liquor retail held by any company. I found no source I could read for them.

- Which award covers a particular hybrid business. Chapter 9 sets out the coverage clauses, and the answer turns on the facts.

- The cyber exposure of each type, beyond chapter 4.

- Any vendor’s prices or fit for a particular business.
