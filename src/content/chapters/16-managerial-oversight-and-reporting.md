Owners who cannot be on site need numbers they trust and a short list of things to check. A dashboard that triggers no decision is noise.

This is a background chapter. It draws on industry practice, so most of it is my read. The one sourced point is about what you can take out of a system. Square’s help centre says: “You cannot print or export cash drawer reports at this time.” and “You cannot export reports from the Transaction status pages – you can only print them.”[^1] If a report you rely on cannot leave the system, it cannot feed your own numbers.

## Five numbers (my read)

- **Venues:** labour as a share of sales, the cost of goods, voids and discounts, covers and average spend, and cash variance.

- **Accommodation:** occupancy, average daily rate, revenue per available room, channel mix and cancellations.

- **Retail:** gross margin by category, stock turn, shrinkage and basket size.

Pick five, review them at the same time each week, and set a threshold for each that triggers a phone call.

## Controls

- Reconcile cash and card every day, and count the fastest-moving items weekly.

- Separate duties, so the person who orders is not the only person who receives.

- Review who can void, refund and edit prices.

- Be clear with staff about cameras and apps. Workplace surveillance rules differ by state and I did not research them. Check yours before pointing a camera at staff.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to your POS, rostering and accounting vendors.

1. Which reports can we export, in what format, and which can we not?

2. Can we pull raw transaction and labour data, by date range, without a limit on size?

3. Which fields are mapped by category, and who maintains the mapping?

## What this chapter does not show

- Any benchmark for any number. I found no source I could read, and vendor benchmarks are marketing.

- The surveillance or privacy law that applies to cameras in your state.

- How AI summaries of variances perform. I tested none.
