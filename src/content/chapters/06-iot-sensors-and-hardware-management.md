A modern venue runs dozens of connected devices: card terminals, printers, cameras, cool room sensors, door locks and smart TVs. Each is a small computer with a login. From 4 March 2026 the Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025 set a security standard for some of them.[^1]

This chapter reads what those Rules cover, which is narrower than it sounds, and what the food safety standard says about the one job sensors are most often sold for.

## What the Rules say

The standard applies to “relevant connectable products” that “are intended by the manufacturer to be used, or are of a kind likely to be used, for personal, domestic or household use or consumption”, and that will be acquired in Australia by a consumer. Desktop computers, laptops, tablets and smartphones are excluded.[^1]

Within that class, the Rules require that passwords be “unique per product” or “defined by the user of the product”. They require the manufacturer to publish “at least one point of contact to allow a person to report security issues”. And they require a published support period for security updates.[^1]

I read the Rules. I did not work out whether a given camera, lock or sensor bought for a venue counts as a consumer product. A product made for business use only may fall outside the class, and the Rules do not tell you which of yours do.

## The cold chain

The temperature rule that most sensors are sold to meet is Food Standards Australia New Zealand’s: “You must ensure that the temperature of potentially hazardous food is either at 5°C or colder or at 60°C or hotter when it is received, displayed, transported or stored.” Cooked food to be cooled must go from 60°C to 21°C in a maximum of two hours, then from 21°C to 5°C within a further four hours.[^2] A logging sensor is one way to show you met it. I did not assess any vendor’s product.

## What I take from it (my read)

- Keep a register of every connected device: what it is, where it is, who owns it, when it was last updated, and when support ends.

- Put the POS, guest Wi-Fi and cameras on separate networks, and change every default password on the first day.

- Prefer devices from manufacturers that publish a support period and a security contact. The Rules require that of consumer devices, and it is a fair question for any device.

- Replace a device that is out of support before it fails.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to each hardware or sensor supplier.

1. Is this product covered by the Cyber Security (Security Standards for Smart Devices) Rules 2025, and if not, what security commitments do you make instead?

2. How are passwords set, and can we change them?

3. Where do we report a security issue, and when will you acknowledge it?

4. For how long will you supply security updates, and what happens to the device when you stop?

5. What happens to the cloud service if the company closes or you discontinue the product?
