CreditorWatch, which sells business credit data, reports that in the 12 months to July 2026 the closure rate for cafés, restaurants and takeaway businesses was 12.03 per cent, against 6.69 per cent across all industries.[^1] It puts it this way: “of every 100 cafés and restaurants trading a year ago, 12 are no longer operating”.[^1]

For a landlord, an empty tenancy loses rent. A tenancy filled with an operator who fails can lose it again, with a fit-out left behind. This chapter reads the one dataset I could verify on operator failure and sets out what a property holder can ask before signing.

## What the data says

The same CreditorWatch release reports that 10.21 per cent of cafés, restaurants and takeaway businesses were 60 or more days in arrears in July 2026, against 5.36 per cent nationally, and that their trade payment default rate was 1.15 per cent against 0.31 per cent.[^1] It reads those as early warnings of closures. That is the vendor’s interpretation of its own data.

The category is cafés, restaurants and takeaway. It does not cover pubs or hotels, and I did not read any equivalent figure for them. The Reserve Bank raised the cash rate target to 4.85 per cent on 3 November 2026, so the cost of finance for an operator’s fit-out has risen too.[^2]

## What I take from it (my read)

- Define what success is before choosing an operator: rent, foot traffic, brand lift or valuation. Each points to a different kind of tenant.

- Prefer operators with proven systems and a cash buffer over those with a good concept and a thin balance sheet.

- Use a short-term licence before committing to a long lease, so both sides can see the trade.

- Put sales and footfall data-sharing in the lease, so performance is visible to both parties.

- Plan the exit if the operator fails: make-good, reletting and signage.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to a prospective operator, or to a landlord if you are the operator.

1. What are the last three years’ trading results, and which systems do you run for sales, stock and labour?

2. What cash is available for the first six months, and who guarantees the lease?

3. What fit-out contribution is offered, what happens to it if the operator leaves early, and who owns the fit-out?

4. What sales and footfall data is shared, how often, and with whom?

5. What are the make-good and reletting terms at exit?

## What this chapter does not show

- Retail vacancy rates. The market report I wanted to cite was not readable from here.

- How landlords such as shopping centre owners are changing their mix. I read no source on that.

- How to structure a lease: turnover rent, management agreements and joint ventures. I did not research the models.

- Failure data for pubs, hotels, bars or bottle shops.
