## Short history

Data residency meant a server in Sydney. Cloud adoption broke that: your SaaS vendor might run in Australia, hold backups in Singapore and have support staff in Manila. Sovereignty is the next step: not where the data sits, but who holds the keys and whose law applies.

## Now

- **Residency is not sovereignty.** A US-owned provider hosting in Sydney can still be compelled by US law.[^1] Vendors increasingly sell "sovereign" offerings. Some are real, some are relabelled, which analysts call sovereignty washing.[^2]

- **Federal rules apply to government**, not you, but they shape the market. The federal government's cloud policy applies to agencies from July 2026.[^3]

- **Vendors respond.** Microsoft says it will offer in-country processing of Microsoft 365 Copilot interactions in Australia, expected by the end of 2026.[^4] Sovereign AI nodes are being built. Survey data on board-level concern is mixed in quality: treat as a signal.

- **Your contracts rarely say.** Most POS, PMS and booking contracts do not state where guest data is stored or processed, or where support staff access it from.

## Next three years

- Tender and landlord requirements start asking where guest data lives.

- Privacy reform changes cross-border disclosure and AI transparency. The Privacy Act now lets the government prescribe countries whose privacy laws are substantially similar to the Australian Privacy Principles. From 10 December 2026, a privacy policy must describe certain automated decisions that could significantly affect a person.[^5]

- Large chains ask vendors for data location and key control in writing. Independents will follow because vendors standardise.

- AI tools raise the stakes: prompts and guest data sent to an overseas model may leave the country.

## Accommodation vs retail

- **Accommodation:** guest ID data, international guests and group chains mean cross-border flows are normal. Ask your PMS vendor where the database is and who administers it.

- **Venues:** booking and loyalty data is the main exposure.

- **Retail bev:** loyalty and ecommerce platforms are usually offshore. Shopify, for one, says it is a Canadian company that may send personal data to other countries, including the United States.[^6] Know it, disclose it in the privacy policy.

## Questions to put to every vendor

1. Where is our data stored, and where is it backed up?
2. Who can access it, from which countries?
3. Who holds the encryption keys?
4. Which foreign laws can compel you to disclose it?
5. What happens to our data on exit, and how fast?

## Risks

- Paying a premium for a sovereignty label that is only residency.

- Assuming the vendor's privacy policy equals your obligation.

- Over-engineering a small venue. The answer for a cafe is a better contract question, not a sovereign cloud.
