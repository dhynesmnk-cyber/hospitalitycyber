Owned content is cheaper than ads and slower than ads. It compounds if you keep at it, and stops if you do not.

This is a background chapter. It draws on industry practice, so most of it is my read. Two points have a source. First, Arts Law says that “AI tools do not currently have a legal status and cannot own copyright”, and that a work needs a human author to be protected.[^1] An image you generate with no human input may not be yours to defend. Chapter 13 covers that. Second, the under-16 account rule is covered in chapter 3.

## A simple plan (my read)

1. Decide how often you can post, and be honest. A rhythm you can keep beats a daily one that stops.

2. Keep a bank of real photos and clips of the food, the people and the room, and refresh it each quarter.

3. One person owns the plan, with a monthly look at what got a response.

4. Build an email list from every booking. It is the one channel that is yours.

5. Answer the specific questions guests search for on your website and your Google Business Profile.

## Rules to check, which I did not research

- Consent from staff and guests filmed on your premises.

- Licences for music and for images you did not make.

- Alcohol advertising codes and the consumer law on misleading images.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to an agency, freelancer or AI content tool.

1. Who owns the copyright in what you produce, and will you assign it to us in writing?

2. Which parts are made with AI, and what do your terms say about commercial use?

3. Who is responsible for music, image and font licences?

4. What do we receive at the end of the engagement, and in what format?

## What this chapter does not show

- What any format, platform or posting rhythm returns for a venue.

- The rules on music licensing, filming consent or alcohol advertising.

- Which social platforms are covered by the under-16 rule.
