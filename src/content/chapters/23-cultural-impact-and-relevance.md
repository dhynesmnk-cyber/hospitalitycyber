A venue that matters to its neighbourhood survives a bad quarter. A venue that does not has only price and location to defend.

This is an editorial background chapter. It is more observation than data, so most of it is my read. The sourced part is about visitors. Tourism Research Australia reports that in the March quarter 2026, international visitors’ spend in Australia was up 15 per cent, nights up 10 per cent and trips up 9 per cent on the same quarter a year earlier.[^1] In the year ending March 2026, holiday travellers spent 57 per cent of their total in Australia on food, drink and accommodation.[^1] Those are shares of spend, not a count of venues or a measure of cultural value.

## What I mean by relevance (my read)

- A venue is part of a place when it hosts something that is not just a meal: a gig, a class, a market, a charity night.

- Guests can tell a venue with a point of view from a generic one. AI-written copy and images make sameness cheap, so a real person and a real place stand out.

- Buying local where it makes sense, and saying so, is a position. Performing a cause you do not live by is a risk, because staff and guests notice.

## What owners can do

1. Say who you are in one sentence, and put it on the website and the menu.

2. Treat staff and neighbours as part of the brand.

3. Use real photography of real people, with their permission.

4. Host something that is not just a meal.

## What this chapter does not show

- Whether cultural programming earns more or protects a venue. I have no data.

- Planning, noise and licensing pressures on small venues.

- How the under-16 social media rule changes how families find venues. Chapter 3 covers what the law says.

- Visitor spend by venue type, or the year-ending dollar total.
