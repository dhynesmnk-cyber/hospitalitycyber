SevenRooms advertises its voice AI with 3,800 calls handled, 850 covers created and $28K of booked revenue. Its own blog says that was one customer, Casper Hospitality, in one month.[^1][^2] On 6 October 2026 Axify, another voice AI vendor, said a pilot across three Laundy Hotels venues “equated to $1.26 million in commercial opportunity plus a further 621 hours returned to teams”.[^3]

Both are vendors’ figures about their own products. This chapter goes job by job through what the public record shows, and where it stops.

## What the record shows

| Job | What the public record shows | My read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Voice agents on the phone | SevenRooms’ figures cover one customer in one month, with no baseline or price.[^2] Axify says at least 30% of calls concern new revenue-related opportunities, from a three-venue pilot.[^3] | Worth a trial on missed calls. Test it yourself before relying on either figure. |
| Drive-thru voice ordering | In Intouch Insight’s 2026 study, voice AI at three US brands took some or all of the order on 92 of 120 visits and “completed 77 from beginning to end”. Accuracy was 90.8%, up from 82.5% in 2025. Nearly three in ten shoppers repeated part of the order.[^4] | A US signal, not an Australian benchmark. Intouch also sells voice AI mystery shopping. |
| Agents booking hotels | Google began hotel booking in AI Mode on 27 August 2026 in the United States, in English, with ten partners.[^5] | Not yet a channel for an Australian venue. |
| Using a vendor’s AI tool | Square’s AI terms give Square an “irrevocable, perpetual, and sub-licensable right” to your AI Content, and let its providers use your data.[^6] | Read the AI terms before the first prompt. |
| Software that decides about people | From 10 December 2026 a privacy policy must describe certain decisions made by a computer program.[^7] | List the tools that score, rank or schedule people. |

As at November 2026. The table describes vendors’ and regulators’ words. It does not rank products.

## Where the evidence is thin

OpenTable’s Australian general manager says voice AI partners help restaurants “capture more booking opportunities”.[^3] That is a statement of intent. The Axify figure is “commercial opportunity”, which is not revenue, and the pilot covered three venues. The SevenRooms page gives no period; the blog supplies “one month”. None of these sources gives a baseline, a price, or an independent measurement.

Intouch’s study measures drive-thrus. Nothing in it measures a venue’s phone line.

## Rules of thumb (my read)

1. Start with tasks where a mistake is cheap.

2. Do not paste guest personal information into an AI tool you have not read the terms of. The Square terms show why: inputs may be shared with the vendor’s providers.[^6]

3. Keep a person on anything that touches money, allergies or a complaint.

4. Ask where the model runs and who sees the prompts. Chapter 5 covers this.

5. Measure before and after. If you cannot measure it, do not pay for it.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to any vendor quoting a figure for an AI product.

1. Over what dates and at how many venues were the figures measured, and who measured them?

2. What was the baseline before the product, and how was the comparison made?

3. What does it cost per venue per month in Australian dollars, and is it available to Australian venues now?

4. Which providers receive our data, in which countries, and may they use it to improve their own services?

5. Does the product make or assist decisions about staff, applicants or guests, and which kinds of personal information does it use?

## What this chapter does not show

- Drafting, image generation, review summarising and demand forecasting tools. I found no evidence I could read for them, so I make no claim.

- Rostering and revenue-management vendors’ AI claims.

- Any independent test of a voice agent in an Australian venue.

- What any of these products cost.
