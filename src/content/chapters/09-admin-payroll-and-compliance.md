From 1 July 2026 an employer must “pay employees their super guarantee for each payday (instead of quarterly)”, and the money must reach the employee’s fund “within 7 business days after paying your employees”.[^1] The same day, modern award minimum rates rose 4.75 per cent and the national minimum wage rose to $26.44 an hour.[^2]

Super guarantee is the super an employer must pay for eligible employees, at 12 per cent. A modern award is the legal minimum pay, loadings and conditions for a type of job, set by the Fair Work Commission. This chapter reads what the ATO and the Commission say, then a point that decides what a venue pays: which award covers which business.

## What the wage decision says

The Commission’s decision of 2 June 2026 says “modern award minimum wage rates will be increased by 4.75 per cent”. The lowest ongoing award rate must be at least $1,004.90 a week, or $26.44 an hour, and the lowest entry-level rate, for no more than the first six months, at least $25.74. The changes take effect for each employee “from the start of the employee’s first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026”.[^2]

The Commission says about 21.1 per cent of employees, almost 2.8 million people, are paid at a modern award minimum rate. Accommodation and food services is one of four industry divisions that account for “over two-thirds of all modern award-reliant employees”.[^2]

## What Payday Super changes

The ATO lists what changes on 1 July 2026:[^1]

- **Timing.** Super must be received by the fund within 7 business days after payday. For a new employee, “your first super contribution for a new employee must reach their super fund within 20 business days after their first payday”.

- **What super is calculated on.** It is 12 per cent of “qualifying earnings, which is a new term that brings together ordinary time earnings and other payments”. That includes commissions and salary sacrifice contributions.

- **Reporting.** “You report both qualifying earnings and super liability through STP”, the Single Touch Payroll reporting that goes to the ATO with each pay run.

- **Late payments.** The super guarantee charge is assessed by the ATO, is “tax deductible”, and “includes interest that compounds daily at the general interest charge rate”. Penalties are “25% or 50% of the unpaid super guarantee charge, depending on any prior penalties”.

- **The clearing house.** The Small Business Superannuation Clearing House closed to new users on 1 October 2025, and from 1 July 2026 “SBSCH is no longer accessible”.

What does not change is who gets super (“eligible employees, including independent contractors paid mainly for their labour”) and the 12 per cent rate.[^1]

The ATO’s worked example is an employer who pays wages fortnightly on a Monday. The wages paid on Monday 6 July 2026 are under the new rules, and their super is due in the fund “by Wednesday 15 July, which is 7 business days after payday”. The June quarter’s super under the old system is still due on 28 July 2026.[^1]

## A café, a pub and a bottle shop: which award is yours?

Which award covers a venue depends on the kind of business, not on the job. Three matter for the venues in this Resource: the Restaurant Industry Award 2020 [MA000119], the Hospitality Industry (General) Award 2020 [MA000009] and the General Retail Industry Award 2020 [MA000004].

**Cafés and restaurants.** The Restaurant Industry Award covers “restaurants, reception centres, night clubs, cafés or roadhouses”. It “does not include a restaurant operated in, or in connection with, premises owned or operated by an employer covered by any of the following awards”, and the first on that list is the Hospitality Industry (General) Award. It also excludes “hotels, motels, hostels and boarding establishments”.[^3]

**Hotels, taverns and similar.** The hospitality industry in the Hospitality Industry (General) Award includes hotels, motels, “wine saloons, wine bars or taverns”, “liquor booths”, resorts and casinos. It also includes “restaurants operating in, or in connection with, premises owned or operated by employers otherwise covered by this award”.[^4]

My reading of the clauses, which is not legal advice: a café on its own premises and a bistro inside a hotel can sit under different awards, although the work is the same.

**Bottle shops.** The General Retail Industry Award leaves “restaurants, cafes, hotels, motels or fast food operations” outside the general retail industry.[^5] It does not mention bottle shops. The Hospitality Industry (General) Award’s classifications include “selling liquor from the bottle department”.[^4] The Commission’s decision, discussing the retail award, refers to selling liquor in a stand-alone bottle shop.[^2] How a particular bottle shop is covered turns on its facts, and I cannot say.

## Same base rate, different penalties

From 1 July 2026 the adult minimum rates in clause 18.1 are identical in the Restaurant Industry Award and the Hospitality Industry (General) Award, at all seven levels.[^3][^4]

| Level | Weekly, full-time | Hourly |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Introductory | $978.10 | $25.74 |
| Level 1 | $1,004.90 | $26.44 |
| Level 2 | $1,029.10 | $27.08 |
| Level 3 | $1,062.90 | $27.97 |
| Level 4 | $1,119.10 | $29.45 |
| Level 5 | $1,189.40 | $31.30 |
| Level 6 | $1,221.10 | $32.13 |

The penalties are not identical. Saturday (125 per cent for full-time and part-time staff, 150 per cent for casuals) and public holidays (225 and 250 per cent) match. These differ:[^3][^4]

| | Hospitality Industry (General) | Restaurant Industry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekday hours with no penalty | 7.00 am to 7.00 pm | 6.00 am to 10.00 pm |
| Weekday late-night loading, $2.95 an hour | 7.00 pm to midnight | 10.00 pm to midnight |
| Weekday early-morning loading, $4.42 an hour | Midnight to 7.00 am | Midnight to 6.00 am |
| Sunday, casual (includes the 25% casual loading) | 175% | 150% at Introductory to Level 2, 175% at Levels 3 to 6 |
| Split-shift allowance | $3.69 a day if the gap is 2 to 3 hours, $5.60 a day if it is longer | $5.60 for each separate work period of 2 hours or more |

Each award’s own pay schedule prints the result for a casual. At Level 1 the Sunday hour is $46.27 under the Hospitality Industry (General) Award and $39.66 under the Restaurant Industry Award.[^3][^4] That is $6.61 an hour apart (my subtraction), for the same work. Applying the wrong award misstates penalties even when the base rate matches.

## What it asks of a payroll tool (my read)

Super moves from a quarterly job to part of every pay run. A rostering or payroll tool has to calculate on qualifying earnings, send the contribution data and the payment on time, and report both figures to the ATO. The ATO says the SuperStream standards were revised to “allow near real-time payments through the New Payments Platform”, and that super funds now have 3 business days to allocate or return contributions.[^1]

The tool also has to know which award applies to which employee. That is a setting someone chose, often at set-up, and it is where the table above can go wrong without any error showing. I read no vendor’s product, so I cannot say which tools do either job.

## What I take from it (my read)

- A rate change is only as good as the date it reaches your payroll tool. Check that the first July pay run used the new rates.

- Find out which award each venue and each employee is mapped to, and who decided. A group with a café, a pub and a bottle shop may need three.

- Spot-check a few staff each month against the award, especially Sunday and late-night hours.

- If you used the government clearing house, confirm you have another route. The ATO says users “must transition to an alternative option”.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to your payroll and rostering vendors.

1. Which award, by name and number (for example MA000119 or MA000009), is each of our venues and each of our staff mapped to, who chose it, and from what date did the 1 July 2026 rates and loadings apply?

2. Does the tool calculate super on qualifying earnings, and how does it pay super with each pay run: through which clearing house or channel, and with what lead time before the 7 business days run out?

3. How does it handle a new employee’s first contribution, and a change of fund?

4. If the tool underpays wages, penalties or super because of an error in its award or super logic, who bears the cost?

5. What do we receive if we leave, and in what format: pay history, super history and award mappings?

## What this chapter does not show

- Which award applies to a particular business. Coverage can turn on facts I have not seen, and this is not legal advice.

- Overtime, junior and apprentice rates, and the other clauses of each award. A venue under another award, such as the Registered and Licensed Clubs Award or the Fast Food Industry Award, or under an enterprise agreement, has different rules.

- Casual conversion, the right to disconnect, contractor classification and state liquor and food licences. I did not research them.

- Which payroll or rostering products meet the Payday Super rules or map awards correctly.
