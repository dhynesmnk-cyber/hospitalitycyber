Between 13 June 2025 and 29 April 2026, three hospitality software businesses changed owner or had a sale agreed: SevenRooms, Olo and Lightspeed’s Upserve line.[^1][^2][^3] Your software contract decides what happens to your data, your price and your exit when that happens to your vendor. This chapter lists the three deals, then the clauses I read in three vendors’ contracts.

## Three deals in under a year

| Date | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| 13 June 2025 | DoorDash “announced it has completed the acquisition of SevenRooms”.[^1] |
| 12 September 2025 | Thoma Bravo “announced the completion of its acquisition of Olo”.[^2] |
| 29 April 2026 | Lightspeed announced the sale of its Upserve U.S. hospitality product line to Skyview Equity for “up to $81 million USD”.[^3] |

Tock is already owned by American Express, which on 15 June 2026 described its dining strategy as building on “successful acquisitions of digital dining platforms Resy and Tock”.[^4]

## Nine clauses worth reading before you sign

| Vendor | Where | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lightspeed | Service Agreement, clause 17 | “Lightspeed may, without Customer’s prior consent, assign its rights and obligations under this Agreement”. You may not assign yours without Lightspeed’s prior written consent.[^5] |
| Lightspeed | Clause 5.3 | You “must use Lightspeed Payments unless otherwise agreed to by Lightspeed in writing”.[^5] |
| Lightspeed | Clause 13.4 | After termination, “Lightspeed may immediately deactivate Customer’s account and delete Customer Content”.[^5] |
| Square | Payment Terms, section 13 | You are solely responsible for “compiling and retaining permanent records of all transactions”.[^6] |
| Square | Payment Terms, clause 33 | “you must not apply a surcharge to any card transaction”.[^6] |
| Square | Generative AI Terms, section II | Square takes an “irrevocable, perpetual, and sub-licensable right” to your AI Content.[^7] |
| SevenRooms | Main Services Agreement, section 4.3 | If you use the DoorDash marketplace, “DoorDash owns all right, title and interest in all Guest Information (including personal information) that is collected independently through DDRMP”.[^8] |
| SevenRooms | Section 11.12 | Updates to the agreement take effect “at least thirty (30) days” after notice, with exceptions for changes in law or from critical service providers.[^8] |
| Square Loans | Repay your loan | The fee “will never change regardless of how quickly or slowly the loan is repaid”.[^9] |


## What I take from them

My read: read three kinds of clause first, because the deals above are the events they govern. Who can assign the contract. What you can export, and when. How much notice you get of a change. The table has an example of each: Lightspeed’s clause 17 on assignment, Square’s section 13 on records, and SevenRooms’ section 11.12 on notice. I read three vendors, and I cannot say what the rest of the market does.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to every vendor before you sign or renew.

1. What is the all-in price for 36 months, including payments, hardware, support and integrations?

2. How do we export all our data, in what format and how fast, and what do you delete when we leave?

3. If you assign our contract or discontinue the product, what notice do we get?

4. Where is our data held, and which third parties can access it?

5. Within how many hours of confirming an incident that touches our data will you tell us?

6. Does any AI feature take a licence to what we put in, or use it to train models?

7. Who is our named escalation contact, and what support hours apply on a Friday night and a Sunday?

## What this chapter does not show

- Renewal terms, cancellation notice, price-rise clauses and support levels. I did not read those clauses.

- Whether Lightspeed’s clause 17 reaches the sale of one product line, as with Upserve. The clause does not name it.

- The contracts of any vendor in the stack table other than Lightspeed, Square and SevenRooms.
