OpenTable says it works with more than 70,000 restaurants worldwide and handles 2 billion seats a year.[^1] American Express completed its purchase of Tock on 15 October 2024, and DoorDash announced an agreement to acquire SevenRooms on 6 May 2025.[^2][^3]

A reservation platform sells a venue bookings and keeps a record of the guests who make them. This chapter reads who owns the main platforms, what one contract says about the guest record, and what I could not source.

## The booking platforms, as the record shows them

| Platform | What the public record shows |
| --- | --- |
| OpenTable | Says it works with “more than 70,000 restaurants worldwide”. That is OpenTable’s own figure, as reported by IT Brief Australia.[^1] |
| Resy and Tock | American Express owns both. On 15 June 2026 it described its dining strategy as building on “successful acquisitions of digital dining platforms Resy and Tock”.[^2] |
| SevenRooms | DoorDash completed its acquisition on 13 June 2025.[^3] |
| Voice agents | SevenRooms shows 3,800+ calls handled for one customer in one month. Axify announced an OpenTable integration on 6 October 2026.[^4][^1] |
| Agents booking hotels | Google’s hotel booking in AI Mode began on 27 August 2026 in the United States, in English, with ten partners.[^6] |

## What a booking contract can say about guests

SevenRooms’ Main Services Agreement, updated 4 February 2026, says in section 4.2 that “Client owns all right, title and interest in, and is solely responsible for Client Information”. Section 4.3 then says that if you use the DoorDash Reservation Marketplace, “DoorDash owns all right, title and interest in all Guest Information (including personal information) that is collected independently through DDRMP”.[^5] I read one vendor’s contract. I did not read OpenTable’s, Resy’s or Tock’s.

## What I take from it (my read)

- Own a booking route that does not depend on a network: your website widget, your phone number, your email list. Know what share of covers comes from each source.

- Know what each booking costs you. Network platforms charge in ways that differ by plan and country, and I could not read current Australian prices.

- Where a marketplace brings the guest, assume the record may not be yours until the contract says it is.

- A voice agent that takes bookings will also take allergy notes and special requests. Check how it records and confirms them.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to each reservation vendor.

1. What do you charge us per booking or per seated cover, by booking source, in Australian dollars, and what notice do we get of a change?

2. Which guest records are ours, which does the vendor or a marketplace own, and can we export all of ours?

3. What share of our covers last quarter came through your network, as against our own channels?

4. If you are sold, or sell the product, what happens to our contract and our guest data?

5. If a voice agent takes a booking, how are allergies and special requests recorded and confirmed back to the guest?
