Google’s documentation for local business structured data includes a worked example for a restaurant. It lists the details a site can mark up so that Google can read them: the restaurant type, the cuisine served, the opening hours by day, and a link to the menu.[^1]

Structured data is a standard way of labelling the facts on a web page so a program does not have to guess them. This chapter is short because most of what is said about websites and AI search is not backed by a source I could read. What follows is what Google itself documents.

## What Google documents

The Local Business page describes how a business can add structured data so Google can show it in features such as a carousel of businesses related to a query. The restaurant example in it includes “servesCuisine”, “openingHoursSpecification” and “menu” fields.[^1] The page is documentation, not a promise that markup will change how a venue appears.

For the Business Profile, Google’s guidelines tell businesses to “Provide your regular customer-facing hours of operation”, and add that you “may also specify special hours for particular days, like holidays or special events”.[^2]

## What delivery and ordering cost

Uber Eats’ Australian merchant pricing page, read on 18 November 2026, lists a 30 per cent marketplace rate when Uber delivers, 16 per cent when the restaurant delivers and 8 per cent for pick-up, and a separate webshop product at 25, 6 and 6 per cent.[^3] Ordering through your own site or point-of-sale order page avoids a delivery platform’s commission, but I have no source for what it costs to run, so I make no comparison.

## What I take from it (my read)

- Own your domain and hosting account in your own name, not your agency’s.

- Publish menus, hours and prices as text on a page, and keep them matched to your Business Profile. I could not confirm how any particular AI product reads a PDF menu, so I would not rely on one.

- Put one booking path, one phone number and one email where a guest or a machine will find them.

- Keep a second administrator on every account, and backups of the site.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to your web agency, builder or ordering provider.

1. Who owns the domain name, the hosting account and the site’s content, and can we have the logins?

2. Does the site publish hours, menu and prices in a form Google documents for structured data?

3. What do online orders and bookings cost us per transaction, and what notice do we get of a change?

4. What do we receive if we leave, and how fast?
