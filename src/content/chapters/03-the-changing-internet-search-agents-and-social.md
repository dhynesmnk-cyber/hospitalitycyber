On 27 August 2026 Google added hotel booking to AI Mode, its conversational search, with ten booking partners. It started in the United States, in English.[^1]

AI Mode is Google’s chat-style search, where a person describes what they want and the system answers in a conversation instead of a list of links. This chapter reads what that launch covers, what one study says about local search, and what the law says about under-16 accounts. Most of what is claimed about AI and search for venues is marketing, and I have marked which findings here are not.

## What Google launched, and where

Google’s announcement, as reported by PPC Land, says that “the hotel or booking platform will act as the merchant of record and handle any customer service”. The rollout started “in the United States in English”, with ten partners arriving over the coming weeks: Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.[^1]

Hotel Online writes that the launch partners leave “independent and boutique hotels visible but unable to book directly”.[^2] That is one trade writer’s reading of the partner list. Australia is not in either report.

## Local search: one study, not about hospitality

Whitespark, a local search company, collected 540 queries in three large US cities (Houston, Phoenix and Denver) across six industries: plumbers, personal injury lawyers, dentists, optometrists, medical clinics and real estate agents. It reports: “On average, AI Overviews appear for 15% of local-intent queries”, and local packs appear for 93%.[^3] A local pack is the map and list of nearby businesses at the top of a Google results page. The study was published in May 2025. It covers no restaurants or hotels and no Australian cities, so it is a signal about local search, not a measure of hospitality.

## Social: the under-16 rule

Section 63D of the Online Safety Act 2021 says a provider of an age-restricted social media platform “must take reasonable steps” to prevent age-restricted users having accounts. The civil penalty is 60,000 penalty units. Under section 63E the requirement takes effect on a day the Minister specifies by notifiable instrument.[^4] I could not open the eSafety Commissioner’s site, so I did not read which platforms are covered or the day the rule took effect.

## What I take from it (my read)

- A machine can only describe a venue from the information it can read. Keep hours, menus, prices, photos and booking links complete and current in your Google Business Profile and on your website.

- Search your own venue in an AI assistant each month and record what it says. Wrong hours or a closed venue are cheap to fix and costly to leave.

- Do not build a promotion that needs an under-16 account.

- Treat any vendor’s claim about AI-driven traffic as marketing until it shows your own numbers.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to your booking engine, channel manager or website provider.

1. Which AI assistants or search products can book our rooms or tables today, in which countries, and through which of your integrations?

2. What does each connection cost, and who is the merchant of record?

3. What data about our venue do you supply to them, and how often is it updated?
