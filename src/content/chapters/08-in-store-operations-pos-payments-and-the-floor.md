## Short history

Cash registers gave way to terminal POS in the 1990s. Cloud POS arrived around 2010 with iPad systems. Lightspeed agreed to buy Kounta, a cloud POS provider for hospitality businesses, in October 2019.[^1] Payments moved from bank terminals to bundled processing: Square, Zeller and Tyro compete with the banks on rate and speed. QR ordering (me&u and others) spread during COVID and settled into a niche for pubs and large venues.

## Now

| Vendor | Fit | Watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lightspeed | Mid-size venues, retail plus hospitality | May require Lightspeed Payments; separate fee on third-party processors |
| Square | Small cafes, simple menus | 1.6% in-person; thin on complex ops |
| Zeller | Cost-sensitive, bank-neutral | Younger ecosystem |
| Tyro | Integrated payments, hospitality focus | Check POS partners |
| TouchBistro, OrderMate, Epos Now, POSApt | Table service, pubs, clubs | Varies by state support |

As at November 2026. Prices change monthly: get a written quote.

Lightspeed's Australian service agreement says an eligible customer “must use Lightspeed Payments unless otherwise agreed to by Lightspeed in writing”, and charges a monthly fee for each month a customer processes transactions through another provider.[^2] Square lists 1.6% per tap or insert on its restaurants pricing page.[^3]

**Surcharges.** From 1 October 2026 the RBA has removed surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa debit, prepaid and credit cards, and lowered the interchange caps on domestic card transactions.[^4] The RBA estimates the changes lower merchants' wholesale card payment costs by around $910 million a year, counting a cap on foreign cards that starts on 1 April 2027.[^5] American Express is not one of the designated networks, so the ban does not cover it. The RBA said it would consider surcharging on American Express in a later review planned for mid-2026.[^5] The RBA estimates that around 16 per cent of merchants surcharged on these cards in 2024/25, so many venues are barely affected, but any venue that relied on surcharge to cover blended pricing should check its statement.[^5]

**Floor tech.** Kitchen display screens, handheld ordering, inventory by recipe and delivery tablet consolidation are standard in mid-size venues. Intouch Insight's 2026 study of 13 US drive-thru chains found AI voice ordering at three brands reached 90.8 per cent order accuracy, up from 82.5 per cent in 2025, against 89.3 per cent across all drive-thru visits. Nearly three in ten shoppers had to repeat part of the order.[^6] These are US chain results, a signal and not a benchmark for an Australian venue.

## Next three years

- Payments and POS merge further. Expect your POS vendor to sell the terminal, the processing, the lending and the payroll.

- Consolidation continues. Lightspeed sold Upserve, its US hospitality product line, to Skyview Equity in April 2026 to focus on its core markets,[^7] and Thoma Bravo agreed to buy Olo in July 2025.[^8]

- Pay-at-table and tap-to-order lift average spend modestly in bars and casual dining, less in fine dining.

- Inventory and waste tracking by camera and scale will arrive at the top end first.

## Accommodation vs retail

- **Accommodation:** the POS must post to the room (PMS integration). Breakfast, bar and minibar are the leak points.

- **Retail food, wine and bev:** age verification, batch and vintage tracking, and loyalty matter more than table management. Lightspeed Retail and Shopify POS fit better than hospitality POS.

## Risks

- Lock-in through payments: leaving the POS means changing processors.

- Fee creep after the first contract term.

- Offline mode that fails on a Friday night. Test it.

- Card data on terminals is a PCI scope issue. Use point-to-point encrypted terminals.
