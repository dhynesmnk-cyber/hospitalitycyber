From 1 October 2026 the Reserve Bank of Australia (RBA) has removed surcharging on eftpos, Mastercard and Visa debit, prepaid and credit cards, and lowered the interchange caps on domestic card transactions.[^1] It estimates the changes lower merchants’ wholesale card payment costs by around $910 million a year, counting a cap on foreign cards that starts on 1 April 2027.[^2]

The point-of-sale (POS) system, the till that records each sale, is where those rules meet your venue. This chapter reads what two POS vendors’ own terms say about payments, and what the one public drive-thru voice AI study measured.

## What the surcharge ban reaches

The ban covers the designated networks only: eftpos, Mastercard and Visa. American Express is not one of them. The RBA said it would consider surcharging on American Express in a later review planned for mid-2026. I did not check what that review decided.[^2]

The RBA estimates that around 16 per cent of merchants surcharged on these cards in 2024/25, and its Payments System Board anticipates those merchants “may increase their advertised prices to cover the cost of accepting card payments”.[^2] So most merchants did not surcharge, and for them the ban changes the card, not the price. My read: a venue that used a surcharge to cover blended pricing should check its statement.

Square’s Payment Terms, updated 1 October 2026, go further than the RBA: “you must not apply a surcharge to any card transaction”. If Square tells you to remove one and you do not within 2 days, it “may suspend your use of the Square Account”.[^3]

## What the vendor’s terms say about payments

Lightspeed’s Australian service agreement says an eligible customer “must use Lightspeed Payments unless otherwise agreed to by Lightspeed in writing”. Clause 5.4 charges a monthly fee for each month a customer processes transactions through another provider.[^4] The Australian fee table runs from $75 to $1,160 a month by estimated volume.[^5] A second clause, for Restaurant POS (U-Series), gives “$/€/CHF 99.00”. The agreement does not say which applies to a restaurant.[^4]

Square’s restaurants pricing page lists “1.6% per tap or insert”. A footnote says 1.6% applies to sellers who signed up on or after 30 May 2024, or who signed up earlier using Square Terminal or Square Register, and that 1.9% applies to other earlier sellers using Square Reader, Square Stand or Tap to Pay.[^6] So the rate depends on when you signed up and on your hardware.

Square’s Australian loans are repaid from card sales, and Square says the fee “will never change regardless of how quickly or slowly the loan is repaid”.[^7]

## Who owns your POS next

On 29 April 2026 Lightspeed announced the sale of its Upserve U.S. hospitality product line to Skyview Equity for “up to $81 million USD”, saying it would “concentrate on its two core growth engines of retail in North America and hospitality in Europe”.[^8] On 3 July 2025 Olo “entered into a definitive agreement to be acquired by Thoma Bravo”.[^9]

My read: when a vendor changes owner, the contract you signed moves with it. Chapter 17 sets out what one vendor’s contract says about that.

## Floor technology: one study with numbers

Intouch Insight’s 2026 drive-thru study, published on 6 October 2026, tested voice AI ordering at three US brands, with data collected from June to July 2026. The AI took some or all of the order on 92 of 120 test visits and “completed 77 from beginning to end”. Its accuracy score was 90.8 per cent, up from 82.5 per cent in 2025. Nearly three in ten shoppers had to repeat part of the order.[^10]

These are US chain results. The study does not say what the 90.8 is measured against, so treat it as a signal, not a benchmark for an Australian venue.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to your POS and payments vendors.

1. Do we have to use your payments product, and what do we pay in a month we use another provider?

2. If you change or discontinue the product we use, what notice do we get, and can we export our data first?

3. How do you identify a surcharge, and when does a notice period start?

4. For any accuracy or completion figure you quote: over how many orders, and who measured it?
