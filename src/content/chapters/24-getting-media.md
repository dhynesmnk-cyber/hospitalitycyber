Earned media is coverage you do not pay for: a news story, a review, a mention in a newsletter. I found no data on how much it moves bookings, and this chapter has no sourced figures.

This is a background chapter. It draws on industry practice, so everything below is my read.

## What gives you a story

An opening, a reopening after a renovation, a new chef, a seasonal menu, an event, a charity tie-in or a milestone. Journalists get many pitches. Short, specific and local ones are the ones I would write.

## A press kit

- Facts: name, address, opening dates, owner and chef, and what is new.

- A handful of high-resolution real photos you own the rights to.

- The owner’s story in a few lines, and one named contact.

Build a list of ten local journalists and creators, and read what they write before you pitch. Pitch one angle at a time to a named person.

## The rules, which I did not research

- Paid or gifted content probably has to be disclosed under consumer law and platform rules. I could not read the regulator’s guidance, so check it before you offer a seat or a sample.

- Alcohol advertising is subject to codes. I did not read them.

- Under-16s are not allowed accounts on certain major platforms. Chapter 3 covers what the law says, and I could not read the list of platforms.

Prepare a holding statement for a bad review, a closure or an incident before you need it.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to a creator, agency or publication you work with.

1. Will the content be marked as paid or gifted, and how?

2. Who approves it before it goes out?

3. What does the arrangement cost, and what exactly do we get?

4. What happens if the creator’s or publication’s behaviour reflects badly on the venue?

## What this chapter does not show
