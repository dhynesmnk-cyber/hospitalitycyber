If the way you run a shift lives in one manager’s head, you do not own the venue’s operations. You rent them from that manager.

This is a background chapter. It draws on industry practice, not dated sources, so everything below is my read and I have no figures to give. A standard operating procedure (SOP) is a written, step-by-step way to do a recurring job, such as opening, cashing up or handling an allergy.

## What to write down (my read)

- Opening and closing, cash handling, responsible service of alcohol, allergens, cleaning, incident reporting, complaints and shift handover.

- The documents that make the business legal and insurable: contracts, licences, insurance, leases, supplier terms, the food safety plan and staff records. In many small venues they sit in someone’s email.

## What good looks like

- One owner for each SOP, with a review date on the first page.

- One page, with photos for physical tasks.

- Training sign-off recorded with the date and the trainer.

- One folder structure for licences, insurance, leases and contracts, and at least two people who can find anything in it.

- A retention rule for each kind of record. Check periods with your accountant and, for staff records, the relevant award. I did not research them.

## The risks

- SOPs nobody reads, written for an auditor.

- Out-of-date allergen information.

- Key documents held only by a manager who is leaving.

- SOPs written by an AI tool and not checked against your state’s rules.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to any checklist, training or document software vendor.

1. Who owns the procedures and records we put in, and can we export all of them, in what format?

2. Where are they held, who at the vendor can see them, and for how long are they kept?

3. If a product feature writes procedures with AI, what checks does it make against local rules, and who is responsible for an error?

## What this chapter does not show

- Any legal retention period, licence condition or training requirement.

- What any tool costs or whether it saves time.

- Whether AI tools write usable SOPs. I tested none.
