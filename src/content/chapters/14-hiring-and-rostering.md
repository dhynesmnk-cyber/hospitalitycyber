Home Affairs reports that at 31 December 2025 there were 179,386 working holiday maker visa holders in Australia, up from 164,055 a year earlier.[^1]

Working holiday makers are visitors on subclass 417 or 462 visas who are allowed to work. My read: they are one source of casual labour for hospitality, but I have no source for what share of venue staff they make up. This chapter covers who is available to hire, what labour costs, and the rule that applies when software screens people.

## Who is available to hire

The same report says 165,782 working holiday maker visas were granted in the six months to 31 December 2025, “compared with 175,070 visa grants in the same period in 2024”.[^1] So the number of holders was higher while the number of new grants was lower. I did not read the report’s breakdown by country or by visa conditions.

## What labour costs

All modern award minimum rates rose 4.75 per cent on 1 July 2026, and the national minimum wage rose to $26.44 an hour.[^2] The Commission describes award-reliant employees as people who “predominantly work part-time hours”, and says “a majority are casuals”. Accommodation and food services is one of four industry divisions that account for over two-thirds of them.[^2] Chapter 9 covers what the rise asks of payroll tools, and which award applies to which kind of venue.

## Where demand comes from

Tourism Research Australia reports that in the March quarter 2026 international visitors’ spend in Australia was up 15 per cent on the same quarter a year earlier. In the year ending March 2026, holiday travellers spent 57 per cent of their total on food, drink and accommodation, and visitors here for employment spent 74 per cent.[^3] The release gives those as shares of spend. I did not find a year-ending dollar total in it.

## When software screens people

From 10 December 2026, the privacy policy of an entity covered by the Australian Privacy Principles must describe certain decisions made, or substantially and directly assisted, by a computer program, where the decision could significantly affect an individual and uses their personal information.[^4] My read: a tool that ranks applicants or scores staff could meet that test. That would be for the vendor’s answers and your own lawyer to settle, not for this chapter.

## What I take from it (my read)

- Record how each hiring decision was made. If software screened or ranked applicants, record the criteria it used.

- Check visa work conditions at hire and again at rehire. I have not read Home Affairs’ rules on conditions.

- Run your award calculation against a manual check of a few staff each month.

## Questions to put in writing

Send these to hiring, rostering and payroll vendors.

1. Does the product rank, score, screen or schedule individuals, and which kinds of personal information does it use for that?

2. Does a person review the result before it takes effect, and can staff or applicants ask why?

3. Which award, by name and number, does it apply to each of our staff, and from what date?

4. What do we receive if we leave, and in what format?
