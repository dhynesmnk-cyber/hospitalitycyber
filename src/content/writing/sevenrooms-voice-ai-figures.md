SevenRooms’ Voice AI page shows three numbers and no dates: “3,800+ CALLS HANDLED”, “850+ COVERS CREATED” and “$28K+ BOOKED GMV”.[^1] SevenRooms’ own blog supplies the period: Casper Hospitality did it “in one month”.[^2] Neither page gives a baseline, a definition of “handled” or a price. My read: without a baseline, the numbers cannot tell you what a voice agent would add at your venues.

## One customer, one month, four US venues

Whose month was it? The blog names it: “Casper Hospitality, which handled more than 3,800 calls and generated $28,000 in booked revenue in one month after switching to SevenRooms Voice AI.”[^2] So the page’s figures describe one group of four venues.

The page quotes Casper’s chief operating officer: “Since switching to SevenRooms Voice AI, we’ve practically retired our phones at all four of our U.S. locations. Every guest call is handled, no missed reservations, no staff interruptions.”[^1]

The same quote appears on the blog with one change. The Voice AI page says “at all four of our U.S. locations”. The blog says “at all four of our locations”. My read: the shorter version drops the one word that tells you where the result came from.

The page also promises: “every caller reaches Voice AI instantly, with no hold music, no waiting and no missed connections.” That is a promise about answering calls. It is not a result about bookings.[^1]

The page labels the dollar figure “BOOKED GMV”. The blog calls it “booked revenue”. Neither says whether cancelled or no-show bookings were deducted.

## What happens on a call, by SevenRooms’ account

The blog describes the sequence: “The guest speaks naturally, the system checks availability, confirms the booking and updates the CRM record automatically”.[^2] The Voice AI page adds that it records calls, so you can “listen to recordings”.[^1]

## $33 a cover, from 22 per cent of calls

My arithmetic: $28,000 across 850 covers is about $33 a cover. 850 covers from 3,800 calls is about 22 per cent of the calls handled. Neither page defines “handled”, so some of those calls may not have been booking requests.

The blog offers its own sum: “Let’s say that a restaurant misses 15 calls per service at a 50% conversion rate and a $90 average check, losing roughly $675 in missed revenue per service.” That is 15 × 50% × $90. It assumes half of missed calls would have become bookings. Casper’s 22 per cent counts all calls handled, not missed calls, so the two are different measures.[^2]

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Use the vendor’s own formula with your own numbers. Count the calls each venue misses per service for a fortnight, estimate the share that would have booked, and multiply by your average check.

2. Ask for a shadow run, where the agent listens and logs what it would have done without answering.

3. Count three things from it: calls that were booking requests, bookings completed with no staff involved, and bookings that were kept. Ask for the cancellation and no-show rates, because a booking made is not a booking kept.

4. Ask where call recordings are stored and for how long. Recordings of guests’ voices are personal information, and the agent writes to the same guest records as everything else, so read [who owns SevenRooms guest data](/writing/sevenrooms-guest-data-doordash/) first.

## What this does not show

- The period beyond “one month”, the baseline before Voice AI, and who measured the results.

- What “handled” and “covers created” mean.

- The price, and whether Voice AI is available to Australian venues. Neither page says.

- How many calls the agent could not complete.

## Put this in writing

Send this to SevenRooms before the figures go into a business case.

> Subject: Evidence for the Voice AI figures on your site
>
> Your Voice AI page shows 3,800+ calls handled, 850+ covers created and $28K+ booked GMV. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Over what dates and at how many locations were these measured?
> 2. How do you define “handled”, “covers created” and “booked GMV”, and are cancelled and no-show bookings deducted?
> 3. What was the call-answering baseline before Voice AI, and who measured the comparison?
> 4. What does Voice AI cost per location per month in Australian dollars, and is it available to Australian venues now?
