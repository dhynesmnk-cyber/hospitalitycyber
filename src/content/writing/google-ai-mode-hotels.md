A guest in the US can now ask Google’s AI Mode for a hotel and finish the booking without leaving Google. Google’s post of 27 August 2026 says hotel booking “has started rolling out in the U.S. in English” with ten partners: “Booking.com, Choice Hotels International, Expedia, Hilton, Hotels.com, IHG Hotels & Resorts, Marriott International, Priceline, Trip.com, and Wyndham Hotels & Resorts.”[^1] Five are hotel chains and five are online travel sites. My read: when an AI does the searching, the businesses in that list sit between your guest and your room.

## From a question to a paid booking

How does it work? Google describes four steps:

1. The guest tells AI Mode “about your upcoming trip and your hotel preferences” and gets “a visual list of options, complete with guest reviews and key factors to compare”.

2. The guest asks AI Mode to complete the booking and selects “Continue on Google”, which appears “next to any of our integrated partners, which include hotel chains and online travel sites”.

3. The guest picks a room type, reviews “details like the cancellation policy”, and pays “securely via Google Pay”.

4. “When you book hotels through AI Mode, the hotel or booking platform will act as the merchant of record and handle any customer service.”[^1]

In plain terms: Google runs the search and the checkout, and the partner takes the booking and the service.

## Who is on the list, and who is not

What kind of business can take the booking? Google’s own words: “hotel chains and online travel sites”.[^1] The five chains are Choice, Hilton, IHG, Marriott and Wyndham. The five travel sites are Booking.com, Expedia, Hotels.com, Priceline and Trip.com.

No independent hotel or regional group is named. The post does not say how a hotel becomes an integrated partner.

One detail stood out to me. Accor appears in the same post, but on a different list: AI Mode will show hotel costs in points, and “In the coming weeks, we’ll also add support for Accor, Flying Blue, Hyatt, LATAM Airlines, and Lufthansa Group.”[^1] It is not among the booking partners.

## US now, and the rest of the world for flights

Is any of this in Australia? Not the hotel booking. The flight feature in the same post is global: “Flight price tracking in AI Mode is now available in more than 180 countries and territories around the world.”[^1] Hotel booking is “in the U.S. in English”. The post gives no date for other countries.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. List which of your properties are sold through any of the ten partners, and in which markets.

2. Check that room types, rates and cancellation policies match across every channel, because AI Mode shows the guest “details like the cancellation policy” from the partner that takes the booking.

3. When AI Mode bookings arrive, report them as their own channel: count, commission and cancellation rate, against your direct bookings.

## What this does not show

- When hotel booking in AI Mode reaches Australia, or whether it will.

- How AI Mode ranks the hotels on its shortlist, or what partners pay Google.

- How a hotel group becomes an integrated partner.

- What guest data Google keeps from a booking paid through Google Pay. I did not read Google Pay’s terms.

Your travel agency partners’ written answers to the email below would settle the first and part of the last.

## Put this in writing

Send this to your account manager at each online travel agency you sell through, and keep the replies.

> Subject: Bookings through Google AI Mode
>
> Google’s post of 27 August 2026 names you as a partner for hotel booking in AI Mode in Search. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Are our properties bookable through your AI Mode integration, in which markets, and from what date?
> 2. What commission or fee applies to a booking made through “Continue on Google”, and is it the same as a standard booking?
> 3. Which guest details do we receive for such a booking, and which does Google receive?
> 4. Will you tell us before the integration reaches Australia?
