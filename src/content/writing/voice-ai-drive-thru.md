In a 2026 US study, 43 of 120 drive-thru orders placed with voice AI did not run from start to end through the AI. That is my arithmetic from Intouch Insight’s own sentence: the technology “took some or all of the order on 92 visits and completed 77 from beginning to end”.[^1] The figure Intouch leads with is different: order accuracy of 90.8 per cent. The page does not say whether that was measured on 120 orders, 92 or 77, and that is the first question for any vendor selling you a voice agent.

## How the test worked: 120 orders at three brands

Who placed the orders? The study “is based on 165 mystery shops per brand across 13 U.S. chains”. Shoppers also “completed 120 Voice-AI orders at three brands”, with data collected from June to July 2026.[^1] The three were Burger King, Taco Bell and Wendy’s, at locations “reported to be using Voice AI”.

My arithmetic: 77 of 120 is about 64 per cent, and 92 of 120 is about 77 per cent. The page does not say what happened on the other 28 visits.

## What improved: accuracy, with the same visit time

“Order accuracy reached 90.8%, up from 82.5% at AI locations in 2025.” Visit time was “essentially even”, at “5:14 at AI locations versus 5:15 at the same brands in the main study”.[^1]

Does 90.8 per cent beat people? The page cannot say. The all-visit figure in the main study was 89.3 per cent, “up 2.4 percentage points from 2025”, but that covers 13 chains and the AI figure covers three brands. Intouch says the AI visits allow “a like-for-like comparison with the same three brands”, and gives that comparison for time and for suggestive selling. It does not give one for accuracy.

## What did not: nearly three in ten repeated themselves

“Nearly three in ten had to repeat part of the order. And while 63% described the AI interaction as easy, 21.7% found it difficult.”[^1] Intouch asks whether voice AI is getting better at taking orders. Its answer: “The short answer is yes, with repetition still creating friction.”

## Intouch sells the measurement it reports

The page ends by offering what Intouch sells: “Targeted mystery shopping for Voice AI drive-thrus can show where the technology performs well, where an employee needs to step in, and what happens at the final handoff.”[^1] My read: the study is useful, and it is not independent of that business.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Before comparing any accuracy figure, get three things: the number of orders it is measured on (120, 92 or 77?), the definition of accurate, and whether staff stepped in. This page states none of them. The full report is behind a download form, and I did not read it.

2. Treat a drive-thru result as a different job from a phone booking. Nothing in this study measures a venue phone line. For a booking agent’s own figures, see [SevenRooms’ 3,800 calls](/writing/sevenrooms-voice-ai-figures/).

3. Run any voice agent in shadow mode on your own calls first, and count how many it completes with no staff involved.

## What this does not show

- What “order accuracy” means, or its base, and how the AI figure compares with the same three brands’ ordinary visits.

- What happened on the 28 visits where the AI did not take part of the order.

- Anything about Australian venues, phone orders or bookings.

- How the 2025 figure was measured. I did not read the 2025 study.

A vendor’s written answers to the email below would settle the first two for that vendor.

## Put this in writing

Send this to any vendor quoting an accuracy or completion figure for a voice agent, before you agree to a trial.

> Subject: Evidence for your voice agent figures
>
> You quote accuracy and completion figures for your voice agent. For our category of venue, over the last three months, please give us in writing:
>
> 1. The number of calls or orders the agent took, how many it completed from start to finish with no staff involved, and how many it handed to staff.
> 2. How you define an accurate order or booking, and the number of orders your accuracy figure is measured on.
> 3. The share of callers who had to repeat part of their request.
> 4. Who measured these figures, and whether we can audit a sample.
