In New South Wales, surveillance of staff “must not commence without prior notice in writing”, and the notice “must be given at least 14 days before the surveillance commences”.[^1] Cameras must be clearly visible, and signs must be “clearly visible at each entrance”. Surveillance that skips these steps is, by the Act’s own definition, covert surveillance, an offence carrying up to 50 penalty units. The ACT has the same 14 days.[^2] My read: a camera analytics rollout is a surveillance project first, and the clock starts with the notice.

## A camera that watches staff is surveillance

Does analytics count? The NSW Act defines camera surveillance as “surveillance by means of a camera that monitors or records visual images of activities on premises or in any other place”.[^1] It does not mention analytics either way. My read: analytics running on a camera still uses a camera that monitors activities, and staff are on the floor.

## What the notice must say

What goes in it? NSW lists five things: the kind of surveillance (camera, computer or tracking), “how the surveillance will be carried out”, when it starts, whether it is continuous or intermittent, and whether it is for a limited period or ongoing. “Notice by email constitutes notice in writing for the purposes of this section.” A new employee must get it “before the employee starts work”.[^1]

The ACT asks for more. Its notice must also state “the purpose for which the employer may use and disclose surveillance records of the surveillance”, and that the worker may consult. Then: “The employer must consult with the worker in good faith about the conduct of the surveillance for not less than the notice period mentioned in section 13 (3).”[^2]

In plain terms: in the ACT, fourteen days is a consultation period, not only a countdown.

## Three states side by side

| | NSW | ACT | Victoria |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Written notice before staff surveillance | At least 14 days[^1] | At least 14 days[^2] | No equivalent rule found[^3] |
| Notice states the purpose of the footage | Not in the list[^1] | Required[^2] | None |
| Consultation with staff | Not in the Act’s notice rules[^1] | In good faith, for the notice period[^2] | None |
| Cameras visible, signs at each entrance | Required[^1] | Required[^2] | No such rule found[^3] |
| Places cameras may never watch staff | Change rooms, toilets, showers and other bathing facilities[^1] | Toilets, change rooms, showers, parent or nursing rooms, prayer rooms, sick bays, first-aid rooms[^2] | Toilets, washrooms, change rooms, lactation rooms[^3] |
| Maximum penalty for those places | 50 penalty units[^1] | 50 penalty units[^2] | 1,200 penalty units for a company[^3] |

## Victoria draws the line elsewhere

What does Victoria require? I searched its Surveillance Devices Act for a written notice rule like NSW’s and found none. Its workplace part bans certain places instead: “an employer must not knowingly install, use or maintain an optical surveillance device or a listening device to observe, listen to, record or monitor the activities or conversations of a worker in a toilet, washroom, change room or lactation room in the workplace.”[^3]

One exception is written for hospitality. The ban does not apply “if required by a condition on a licence granted under the Liquor Control Reform Act 1998”.[^3] That detail stood out to me: a liquor licence can require a camera the Act otherwise bans.

Outside those rooms, a person “must not knowingly install, use or maintain an optical surveillance device to record visually or observe a private activity to which the person is not a party”, without consent. An activity is not private where the parties “ought reasonably to expect that it may be observed by someone else”.[^3]

## Where analytics changes the notice

Does switching on analytics need a fresh notice? The Acts do not say. NSW’s notice must cover “how the surveillance will be carried out”, and the ACT’s must state the purpose of using the footage.[^1][^2] NSW also limits how records may be used, to purposes including “a legitimate purpose related to the employment of employees of the employer or the legitimate business activities or functions of the employer”.[^1]

My read: software that times staff, counts their movements or flags behaviour changes both how and why. Treat it as new surveillance, and give new notice.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. List every camera that can see staff, by venue and state, with the purpose each one serves.

2. Before an analytics feature goes live, write the notice: kind, how, when, continuous or intermittent, and for how long. In the ACT, add the purpose and the right to consult. Send it 14 days ahead, by email in NSW if that suits.

3. Check every camera against the banned rooms in each state you trade in.

4. Ask the vendor where footage and analytics outputs are kept, for how long and who can see them. A system that scores staff may also meet [the privacy duty that starts on 10 December](/writing/privacy-act-automated-decisions/).

## What this does not show

- Queensland, Western Australia, South Australia, Tasmania and the Northern Territory. I read NSW, the ACT and Victoria only.

- Whether a particular analytics product is surveillance under each Act. That is a legal question, and this is not legal advice.

- Consultation duties in awards or enterprise agreements. I did not read them.

- What a penalty unit is worth today. Each Act sets penalties in units, and their dollar values change.

## Put this in writing

Send this to your camera or analytics vendor before installation, and use the reply to write the staff notice.

> Subject: Your camera analytics and our staff notice
>
> Before we switch on your analytics at our venues, please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. What does the system observe, record or infer about staff, as distinct from guests?
> 2. Is it continuous or intermittent, and can it be limited to areas where staff do not work?
> 3. Where are footage and outputs stored, for how long, and who can access them?
> 4. Will you confirm that no camera in our installation covers a toilet, washroom, change room or lactation room?
