From 10 December 2026, Australian Privacy Principle 1.7 says an APP entity’s privacy policy “must contain” information about some decisions made by software.[^1] The test is whether a “computer program” makes a decision, or does a thing “substantially and directly related to making” one, that could significantly affect an individual, using their personal information. The Act does not use the words “artificial intelligence”. My read: a rostering or screening rule set up years ago can be in scope, as much as a new AI tool. This is not legal advice.

## Two years after assent, to the day

Where does the date come from? The Act was assented to on 10 December 2024. Its commencement table starts Schedule 1, Part 15 on “The day after the end of the period of 24 months beginning on the day this Act receives the Royal Assent”, and gives the date: 10 December 2026.[^1] The Federal Register already lists a version of the Privacy Act 1988 that starts that day.[^2]

## Three tests decide whether the duty applies

The Act’s Schedule 1, Part 15 adds APP 1.7 to 1.9 to the Privacy Act 1988. APP 1.7 applies if all three of these hold:[^1]

1. “the entity has arranged for a computer program to make, or do a thing that is substantially and directly related to making, a decision”;

2. “the decision could reasonably be expected to significantly affect the rights or interests of an individual”; and

3. “personal information about the individual is used in the operation of the computer program to make the decision or do the thing that is substantially and directly related to making the decision”.

Miss any one and APP 1.7 does not apply.

APP 1.9 widens what counts. A decision includes “refusing or failing to make a decision”. It counts whether the rights or interests are “adversely or beneficially affected”.[^1] In plain terms: a refusal counts, and so does a good outcome.

Its examples include “a decision that affects the individual’s rights under a contract, agreement or arrangement” and “a decision that affects the individual’s access to a significant service or support”. They are examples only.[^1]

## What your policy then has to say

APP 1.8 asks for three things: “the kinds of personal information used in the operation of such computer programs”, “the kinds of such decisions made solely by the operation of such computer programs”, and “the kinds of such decisions for which a thing, that is substantially and directly related to making the decision, is done by the operation of such computer programs”.[^1]

So the policy describes kinds: the kinds of information a program uses, and the kinds of decision it makes alone or helps make.

## Systems you installed years ago count too

Item 89 applies the duty to decisions made after commencement, whether the arrangement for the program “was made before or after that commencement”. It says the same of when personal information was used, acquired or created.[^1] My read: a system you installed years ago is in scope for decisions it makes from 10 December 2026.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. List every system that scores, ranks, approves, blocks or schedules a person. In a venue group that could include rostering, applicant screening, fraud and chargeback tools, ban lists and no-show scoring on reservations.

2. For each, record the kinds of personal information it uses.

3. Record whether a person reviews the result before it takes effect. That separates the decisions a program makes alone from those it helps make, which is the split APP 1.8 asks for.

4. Include rules-based tools. The text does not require machine learning, so a program that meets the three conditions is in the same position.

## What this does not show

- Whether your group is an APP entity. That definition, and any exemptions, are in the Privacy Act 1988 itself, which I did not read.

- Later amendments. This is the text as made. The Federal Register lists a version of the Privacy Act 1988 from 10 December 2026 but, when I checked, published no text for it.[^2]

- What “significantly affect” means in practice. I read no guidance from the privacy regulator.

- Which of your systems meet the test. The examples in APP 1.9 are examples only.

## Put this in writing

Send this to each software vendor that holds staff, applicant or guest data, before 10 December.

> Subject: Automated decisions and our privacy policy
>
> From 10 December 2026, APP 1.7 in Schedule 1 to the Privacy Act 1988 may require our privacy policy to describe decisions made, or substantially and directly assisted, by a computer program. For each product you supply to us, please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Does the product make, or do anything substantially and directly related to making, a decision about an individual, such as a staff member, applicant or guest?
> 2. What kinds of personal information does it use for that purpose?
> 3. Which kinds of decisions does it make solely, and which does it only assist?
> 4. Who will tell us when the answers change?
