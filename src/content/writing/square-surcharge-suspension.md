A card surcharge left on at one venue can now get your Square account suspended. Square’s Payment Terms, updated 1 October 2026, say you “must not apply a surcharge to any card transaction”, and put the checking on you: “It is your responsibility to ensure that you have implemented processes and procedures so that no surcharging on card transactions occurs from this time.”[^1] The RBA’s own ban covers three card networks.[^2] Square’s covers any card.

## Before and after 1 October: may surcharge, then must not

What changed? Until 1 October, the same clause allowed it: “Until 1 October 2026, you may choose to apply a surcharge to a card transaction, to the extent permitted by Law.” From that day it forbids it.[^1]

The page header lists the key changes. The first is surcharging. The second is “an increase to the Instant Transfer fee from 1.5% to 1.95%”.[^1]

## Notice, two days, suspension, termination

What happens if Square finds one? Clause 33 sets out six steps:

1. Square identifies a surcharge: “If we identify that you are applying a surcharge to card transactions from 1 October 2026”.

2. It notifies you: “we will notify you that this is not permitted”.

3. If you do not act within 2 days, it may suspend the account: “if you do not remove the surcharge within 2 days, we may suspend your use of the Square Account.”

4. While suspended: “You will not be able to use the Services while your Square Account is suspended”.

5. It may reinstate you: “we may agree to reinstate your use of the Square Account with your acknowledgement of this clause 33”.

6. If you then continue: “we may terminate your Square Account without prior notice”.[^1]

Step 4 says “the Services”, not card payments. My read: that points to more than the card feature, but check the definition in the General Terms.

## Wider than the RBA

The RBA decided on 31 March 2026 on “removing surcharging on debit, prepaid and credit cards on the designated eftpos, Mastercard and Visa card networks”, with the change taking effect on 1 October 2026.[^2] Its conclusions paper adds: “Some merchants were concerned that they may lose the ability to surcharge American Express cards. This issue will be considered as part of the next RBA review planned for mid-2026”.[^3]

So the regulator names three networks and left American Express for later. Square’s clause says “any card transaction”.

The RBA’s Payments System Board anticipates that the 16 per cent of merchants that currently surcharge “may increase their advertised prices to cover the cost of accepting card payments”.[^3]

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Search every place a surcharge can sit: POS surcharge settings, online and QR ordering apps, payment links, invoices and event deposits.

2. Look for fee lines in item-level sales exports.

3. Name who owns each setting, so a notice from Square reaches someone who can act inside 2 days.

I have not tested how Square detects a surcharge. The clause says only “If we identify”.

## What this does not show

- How Square identifies a surcharge, and what counts as one. The clause does not say whether a booking fee, a service charge or a fee added by a third-party app is covered.

- Whether the 2 days run from Square’s notice or from its identifying the surcharge.

- What happened to American Express surcharging. The RBA’s list of 2026 media releases, read on 17 November 2026, shows no decision on it.[^4]

- Whether other payment providers have the same clause. I did not read them.

Square’s written answer to the email below would settle the first two.

## Put this in writing

Send this to Square now, before any notice arrives, and keep the reply.

> Subject: Surcharging under clause 33 of the Payment Terms
>
> Clause 33 of your Payment Terms (last updated 1 October 2026) says we must not apply a surcharge to any card transaction. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Does “any card transaction” include American Express and other cards outside the RBA’s designated networks (eftpos, Mastercard and Visa)?
> 2. How does Square identify a surcharge, and does a service charge, a booking fee or a fee added by a third-party app count?
> 3. When do the 2 days start: when Square identifies the surcharge, or when it notifies us? How will you notify us, and whom?
> 4. During a suspension, which Services stop, and is payout of our Proceeds affected?
