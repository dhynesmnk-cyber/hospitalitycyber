Clause 17 of Lightspeed’s Australian agreement runs one way. You “may not assign” your rights “without Lightspeed’s prior written consent”. Lightspeed “may, without Customer’s prior consent” assign its own to a company it merges with, a parent or subsidiary, or the buyer of “all or substantially all its stock/interest or assets”.[^1] The clause does not say you are told first. If the new owner then changes or drops the product, you can leave without penalty only inside the notice period, and after termination your data can be deleted.

## Your way out lasts only as long as the notice period

What can a new owner do once it holds your contract? Clause 12.2 lets Lightspeed “add to, remove, change or discontinue the Products or any component or version of the Products”. If you do not accept a notified change, “Customer has the right to terminate the Agreement without penalty by notifying Lightspeed in writing within the notice period”. Clause 13.4 says that after termination “Lightspeed may immediately deactivate Customer’s account and delete Customer Content”.[^1]

In plain terms: object inside the notice period or lose the penalty-free exit, and once you leave, your data can be deleted at once.

## Three deals in under a year

How often do hospitality software vendors change hands? Often enough to plan for. Tock is already owned by American Express, which on 15 June 2026 said its dining strategy builds on “successful acquisitions of digital dining platforms Resy and Tock”.[^2] Three deals since:

- 13 June 2025: DoorDash “announced it has completed the acquisition of SevenRooms”.[^3]

- 12 September 2025: Thoma Bravo “announced the completion of its acquisition of Olo”.[^4]

- 29 April 2026: Lightspeed announced “the sale of its non-core Upserve U.S. hospitality product line” to Skyview Equity for “up to $81 million USD”. The sale covered “~3,200 U.S. hospitality Customer Locations”.[^5]

In that release Lightspeed said it would “concentrate on its two core growth engines of retail in North America and hospitality in Europe”.[^5] The agreement quoted here is its Australian one. My read: Australian hospitality is not one of the two engines named.

## What happens to you when a vendor changes hands

1. The vendor merges, sells itself, or reorganises.

2. Under clause 17, Lightspeed may assign your agreement to the entity that results. The clause does not say you are told first.

3. The new owner may change or discontinue the product under clause 12.2, on “reasonable notice” that Lightspeed will “endeavour” to give.

4. If you reject a notified change, you may leave without penalty, but only within the notice period.

5. On termination, your account may be deactivated and your content deleted.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Keep a contract register for every vendor that holds guest or sales data: its assignment clause, the notice it gives of product changes, and what you can export on exit.

2. Review the register whenever a vendor announces a deal.

3. Hold a monthly export yourself. Clause 13.4 puts deletion after termination, and 12.2 can require “migrating to an alternative Product”.[^1] Your own copy is the only one that depends on neither.

Before a Lightspeed renewal, read [its processor fee clause](/writing/lightspeed-processor-fee/) too.

## What this does not show

- Clause 17 names mergers, group companies and a sale of all or substantially all stock or assets. It does not name the sale of one product line, as with Upserve, and the agreement does not say how that would be treated.

- What Upserve customers were told, or what their contracts said. The release does not say.

- How other vendors’ contracts handle assignment. I did not compare them.

Lightspeed’s written answer to the email below would settle the first point as it affects you.

## Put this in writing

Send this to Lightspeed before you renew, and keep the reply.

> Subject: Assignment and product changes under our Service Agreement
>
> Your Service Agreement (last updated 26 February 2026) lets Lightspeed assign it without our consent under clause 17. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. If Lightspeed assigns our agreement, how much written notice will we receive, and from whom?
> 2. If you discontinue the product or version we use under clause 12.2, what is the minimum notice, and will you provide a full export of our data before any deletion under clause 13.4?
> 3. Is Australian hospitality a core market for Lightspeed, and will you commit to that in writing for our term?
