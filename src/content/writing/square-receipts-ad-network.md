A guest who asks for a digital receipt at your venue may later see ads built from that purchase. Square’s buyer privacy notice, last updated 15 July 2026, lists among its uses “the email address or phone number you provide when receiving a digital receipt, the transaction details and other details included in your digital receipt”, used “as part of our commerce media network to deliver personalised advertisements”.[^1] It names three advertising partners. The seller contracts I searched do not mention the network at all.

## The receipt, the amount, the card, and three ad firms

What goes in? The notice’s own examples are “details about the Seller, transaction amount and currency, and payment method”, along with the guest’s receipt email or phone number. The ads can come “from us and our affiliates (e.g., Afterpay), and third parties across the internet”.[^1]

Its section on sharing names the firms: “In addition, limited information (e.g., email address/phone number and transaction details) may also be shared with our advertising partners which include Yahoo Ad Tech Australia Pty Ltd, Magnite Inc. and Narrative I/O Inc, and our group companies as part of our commerce media network to deliver personalised advertisements to you across the Internet.”[^1]

In plain terms: the receipt is also an advertising signal, and it can leave Square.

## Your venue’s name and sales go in too

The notice that applies to you as a seller has its own version: “We may also share limited information about your business (i.e. business location and name, and purchase information on Buyer receipts) with our group companies and our advertising partners”, the same three firms, “for the purposes of facilitating personalised advertising to Buyers within our commerce media network”.[^2] The group companies are Square’s “other business units (Cash App, Afterpay/Clearpay, and TIDAL)” and the “wholly-owned subsidiaries of Block, Inc.”[^2]

Can you switch it off for your venues? The seller notice describes one advertising opt-out, and it concerns your own browsing: “You can exercise your right to opt-out of targeted advertising through the ‘Your Privacy Choices’ control or a similar opt-out preference setting within the browser you use to access our website.”[^2] It describes no way to take your venues out of the network.

I searched Square’s General Terms, Payment Terms and Additional Point of Sale Terms for “commerce media”, “advertising partner” and “personalised advert”. None of the three phrases appears in any of them.[^3][^4][^5]

## Guests can opt out. Sellers are not told how

Square runs an opt-out page for guests. It says “Square AU Pty Ltd collects, uses, and shares your email address, phone number, and transaction data for digital receipts from Square sellers, as well as to offer personalised online advertising from Square, our affiliates (like Afterpay), and partners.” A guest enters an email or phone number to opt out, and Square adds: “We’ll retain your information for other approved purposes, including sending you digital receipts.”[^6]

The buyer notice also says Square keeps some sellers out, “by excluding certain kinds of Sellers from this program, such as certain healthcare providers”.[^1] So Square decides which sellers are in. My read: it can tell you whether yours are.

## How a receipt becomes an ad

1. A guest pays at your venue and asks for a receipt by email or text.

2. Square sends the receipt.

3. Square may use that email or phone number, with the transaction details, in its commerce media network.[^1]

4. It may share limited information with Yahoo Ad Tech Australia, Magnite, Narrative I/O and its group companies.[^1]

5. The guest may see personalised ads across the internet, from Square, Afterpay or third parties, unless they have opted out.[^1][^6]

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Work out how many guests get a digital receipt at your venues each month. That is the pool in scope.

2. Treat receipt contacts as shared. The same email address can sit in your CRM and in Square’s network at once.

3. Read your own guest privacy policy beside Square’s, and check what it tells guests about receipts. This is not legal advice.

4. When you export receipt data for your own models, record where each contact came from. Keeping that copy is [your job under Square’s terms](/writing/your-square-data/).

## What this does not show

- Whether your venues are in the network today. The notice names only “certain healthcare providers” as an excluded kind of seller.

- What “purchase information” and “transaction details” cover, item by item. The examples stop at the seller, the amount, the currency and the payment method.

- What the three partners may do with the data. I did not read their terms.

- Whether a seller can opt out.

- Any effect on your own privacy obligations. This is not legal advice.

Square’s written answer to the email below would settle the first, second and fourth.

## Put this in writing

Send this to Square before your next guest-marketing or AI project uses receipt data, and keep the reply.

> Subject: Our venues and your commerce media network
>
> Your Privacy Notice for Buyers and Users (last updated 15 July 2026) and your seller privacy notice (last updated 17 September 2026) describe a commerce media network that uses digital receipt details for personalised advertising. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Are our venues part of the network in Australia, and since when?
> 2. Which receipt fields reach each named partner (Yahoo Ad Tech Australia, Magnite and Narrative I/O) and your group companies?
> 3. How do we take our venues out of the network, and what changes for our guests if we do?
> 4. Can ads shown to our guests through the network come from other businesses, including other venues?
