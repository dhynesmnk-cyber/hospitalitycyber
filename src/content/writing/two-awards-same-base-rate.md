Put a Level 1 casual on a Sunday shift and the award decides whether the hour costs $46.27 or $39.66.[^1][^2] The Hospitality Industry (General) Award and the Restaurant Industry Award print those figures in their own pay schedules, while paying the same adult base rate at every level. Which award applies depends on what kind of business runs the venue. My read: a payroll tool set to the wrong award would misprice Sundays and late nights while every base rate looks right.

## The base rate is the same at every level

Clause 18.1 of each award sets the same seven adult hourly rates: $25.74 at Introductory, then $26.44, $27.08, $27.97, $29.45, $31.30 and $32.13 at Level 6.[^1][^2] The Commission’s wage decision says the new rates take effect for each employee “from the start of the employee’s first full pay period on or after 1 July 2026”.[^3]

## Where the penalties part ways

If the base rates match, where is the gap? Saturday (125 per cent for full-time and part-time staff, 150 per cent for casuals) and public holidays (225 and 250 per cent) are the same in both awards. These are not:[^1][^2]

| | Hospitality Industry (General) | Restaurant Industry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weekday hours with no penalty | 7.00 am to 7.00 pm | 6.00 am to 10.00 pm |
| Weekday late-night loading, $2.95 an hour | 7.00 pm to midnight | 10.00 pm to midnight |
| Weekday early-morning loading, $4.42 an hour | Midnight to 7.00 am | Midnight to 6.00 am |
| Sunday, casual (includes the 25% casual loading) | 175% | 150% at Introductory to Level 2, 175% at Levels 3 to 6 |
| Split-shift allowance | $3.69 a day if the gap is 2 to 3 hours, $5.60 a day if it is longer | $5.60 for each separate work period of 2 hours or more |

Each award’s own pay schedule prints the casual Sunday figures: $46.27 under the Hospitality Industry (General) Award and $39.66 under the Restaurant Industry Award, $6.61 an hour apart by my subtraction.[^1][^2] An illustration, not a claim about any venue: 40 casual Level 1 hours on a Sunday is $264.40 apart, or about $13,750 over 52 Sundays (my arithmetic).

The Commission says award-reliant employees “predominantly work part-time hours” and “a majority are casuals”, across all awards.[^3]

## A stand-alone café and a bistro inside a hotel

Which award applies? The Restaurant Industry Award covers “restaurants, reception centres, night clubs, cafés or roadhouses”. It “does not include a restaurant operated in, or in connection with, premises owned or operated by an employer covered by any of the following awards”, and the first on that list is the Hospitality Industry (General) Award.[^1]

That award’s hospitality industry includes hotels, “wine saloons, wine bars or taverns” and “restaurants operating in, or in connection with, premises owned or operated by employers otherwise covered by this award”.[^2]

My reading of the clauses, which is not legal advice: a stand-alone café and a bistro inside a hotel can sit under different awards. Where an employer is covered by more than one award, each says an employee is covered by “the award containing the classification that is most appropriate to the work performed by the employee and the industry in which they work”.[^1][^2] The Commission’s decision says Accommodation and food services is one of four industry divisions that account for “over two-thirds of all modern award-reliant employees”.[^3]

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Ask for the award setting by name and number for each venue and each employee group, with the date it took effect.

2. Run three test shifts for a Level 1 employee in each venue. In each pair below, the first figure is the Hospitality Industry (General) Award and the second the Restaurant Industry Award:
   - A casual’s Sunday hour: $46.27 or $39.66.
   - A part-timer’s hour from 7.00 pm to 8.00 pm on a Wednesday: $29.39 or $26.44. My arithmetic: $26.44 plus the $2.95 loading.
   - A part-timer’s hour from 6.00 am to 7.00 am on a Tuesday: $30.86 or $26.44. My arithmetic: $26.44 plus the $4.42 loading.

3. If an answer matches the other award, the mapping is wrong. Test split shifts separately, because the two allowances are worked out differently.

4. When you export payroll data, keep the award name, number and effective date with each employee, so a change shows.

A rostering tool that decides shifts may also meet [the new privacy duty from 10 December](/writing/privacy-act-automated-decisions/).

## What this does not show

- Which award covers a particular business. Clause 4 turns on facts I have not seen, and this is not legal advice.

- Overtime, junior and apprentice rates, and the other allowances. A venue under another award, such as the Registered and Licensed Clubs Award, or under an enterprise agreement, has different rules.

- Any payroll or rostering product. I tested none, so I do not know whether any maps awards wrongly.

- Whether the awards change again. Both texts are consolidated up to and including 1 July 2026.

## Put this in writing

Send this to your payroll or rostering vendor before the next pay run you can check.

> Subject: Award mapping and penalty rates in our account
>
> At their 1 July 2026 rates the Restaurant Industry Award 2020 [MA000119] and the Hospitality Industry (General) Award 2020 [MA000009] have the same adult base rates but different penalty rules. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Which award, by name and number, is each of our venues and each employee group mapped to, who set it, and from what date?
> 2. For a Level 1 employee under each award, what does the tool pay for a casual’s Sunday hour, for a part-timer’s hour from 7.00 pm to 8.00 pm on a Wednesday, and for a part-timer’s hour from 6.00 am to 7.00 am on a Tuesday, and how does it apply the split-shift allowance? Please show the clause the tool applied.
> 3. When an award changes, how are we told, and from what date does the tool apply it?
> 4. If the tool applies the wrong award, who bears the back pay or the overpayment?
