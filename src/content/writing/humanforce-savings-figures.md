Humanforce gives two figures for what its rostering saves. Its Smart Scheduling page, for the AI forecasting and roster tools it describes as “currently rolling out in early access”, shows 15% beside “Labour cost savings”.[^1] Its hospitality page shows 25% beside the same words.[^2] Both pages show 70% beside “Faster rostering”. Neither says what the savings were measured against, over what period, or for which customers. My read: a business case needs those three answers before it needs either number.

## Two pages, two savings figures, one speed figure

What exactly do the pages say? The Smart Scheduling page opens: “AI-powered Smart Scheduling for frontline industries, currently rolling out in early access.” Beside the 70% and 15% it lists 100% award verified and over 10 million shifts a month, and says Humanforce is “Trusted by 1M+ frontline workers at Flight Centre, Hoyts, Mitre 10, Accor Group, and more”.[^1]

The hospitality page says “Trusted by 60,000+ hospitality workers across Australia”, and lists more than 190 venues, a shift fill time under five minutes and 25% labour cost savings. Under “Demand-Based Rostering” it repeats 70% faster rostering and 25% “Labour savings”.[^2]

In plain terms: the hospitality page carries the larger savings figure beside the same speed figure. Neither page attaches a footnote, a date or a sample size to any of its numbers.

## What the AI is meant to do

How would the savings arise? Humanforce describes the sequence: “Analyse sales, footfall, and historical data to predict staffing needs”, then “Early-access AI tools help build optimised schedules balancing cost, compliance, and preferences”, then “Managers retain full visibility and final publishing control”.[^1] The hospitality page says rosters are built “based on predicted covers, bookings, and historical patterns”.[^2]

The rostering product page shows a forecast set-up screen with three optimisation models. “Baseline” is to “Follow the forecast demand without optimisation”. “Service Optimised” is to “Prioritise coverage against expected demand”. “Cost Optimised” is to “Prioritise labour cost within coverage”.[^3]

My read: a savings figure means little until you know which of those settings produced it, and against what.

## The award the page names

The hospitality page lists “Hospitality Award 2020”, with “Pre-configured award interpretation with all classifications, penalties, and allowances for every venue type.”[^2] It does not say whether other awards are configured.

My read: a group with a stand-alone café or restaurant should ask which award the tool applies there, because [two awards with the same base rate pay different penalties](/writing/two-awards-same-base-rate/). A labour saving measured on the wrong award is not a saving.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Ask for the basis of each figure: how many customers and venues, over what period, against what baseline, and with which optimisation model.

2. Run a shadow forecast. Give the tool four weeks you have already traded and compare its forecast and roster with what actually happened.

3. Measure labour cost as a share of sales, venue by venue, before and after, with the award settings checked first.

4. Treat early-access features as subject to change, and keep the roster you would have built without them for comparison.

## What this does not show

- How either figure was measured, and whether 15% and 25% describe the same thing.

- Whether the 25% hospitality figure comes from the AI tools, which are in early access, or from Humanforce’s rostering in general.

- What Smart Scheduling costs. I did not read Humanforce’s pricing.

- Other rostering vendors’ claims. This piece reads one vendor.

Humanforce’s written answer to the email below would settle the first three.

## Put this in writing

Send this to Humanforce before either figure goes into a business case, and keep the reply.

> Subject: Basis for the labour cost savings on your website
>
> Your Smart Scheduling page shows 15% labour cost savings, and your hospitality page shows 25%. Please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. For each figure, how many customers and venues, over what period, and against what baseline?
> 2. Which product and which optimisation model produced each figure, and were the early-access AI tools part of it?
> 3. Which award does the tool apply to a stand-alone café, as distinct from a restaurant inside a hotel?
> 4. What does Smart Scheduling cost per location or per employee, and is it included in our current plan?
