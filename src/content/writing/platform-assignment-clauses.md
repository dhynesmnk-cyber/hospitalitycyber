Your booking or ordering platform can change owner without a word of your contract changing, because most of these contracts already allow for it. Tock’s merchant terms say: “Tock may assign these Terms or any Order Form at any time without Merchant's consent.”[^1] SevenRooms, Lightspeed and Olo’s terms for its Omnivore software allow assignment without consent on a merger or sale.[^2][^3][^4] Square’s terms bar you from assigning yours and are silent on Square’s.[^5] My read: the clause that matters at renewal is the one most groups never read.

## Four new owners in about 18 months

How often does this happen? Four times since October 2024:

- 15 October 2024: American Express “completed the previously announced acquisition of Tock”.[^6]

- 13 June 2025: DoorDash “announced it has completed the acquisition of SevenRooms”.[^7]

- 12 September 2025: Thoma Bravo “announced the completion of its acquisition of Olo”.[^8]

- 29 April 2026: Lightspeed announced “the sale of its non-core Upserve U.S. hospitality product line”, covering “~3,200 U.S. hospitality Customer Locations”.[^9]

The releases do not say what changed for customers. The contracts below say what could.

## The five clauses side by side

| Platform, and its owner since the deal | Can the vendor assign without your consent? | Can you? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tock (American Express since October 2024) | Yes, “at any time”[^1] | Only to a successor to substantially all of your business[^1] |
| SevenRooms (DoorDash since June 2025) | On a merger, consolidation or sale of substantially all its stock or assets, with notice “promptly following”[^2] | Yes, on the same terms[^2] |
| Lightspeed (Australian agreement) | To a merger partner, a parent or subsidiary, or the successor in a sale of substantially all of it[^3] | Not without Lightspeed’s consent[^3] |
| Olo, Omnivore software (Thoma Bravo since September 2025) | To a buyer of substantially all, or an affiliate, if the assignee accepts the obligations in writing[^4] | Yes, on the same terms[^4] |
| Square (Australian terms) | The terms do not say[^5] | No: an attempt is “null and void”[^5] |

In plain terms: in a sale, four of the five let your contract move without your say. Only Tock allows it at any time, sale or not.

## Tock: any time, and new terms take effect at once

What can Tock’s owner do? Two clauses work together. Assignment first. You “shall not directly or indirectly assign, sell or otherwise dispose of these Terms or any Order Form, in whole or in part, without the prior written consent of Tock”, with one exception, for “a successor in interest to substantially all of the business of Merchant”. Tock’s own right, quoted above, has no condition at all.[^1]

Then amendments: “We may modify, amend, or alter these Terms at any time in our sole discretion”, and the revised terms “will take effect immediately and apply to your use of the Services from that date on”.[^1]

In plain terms: Tock can change both who you contract with and what the contract says, without asking first.

The data clause is in your favour: “Tock acknowledges and agrees that as between Tock and Merchant, Merchant shall be the sole and exclusive owner of all right, title and interest in and to the Merchant Data.”[^1]

Leaving has a cost the other contracts do not spell out. On termination, future offerings “shall be canceled by, and removed from, Tock as of such termination or expiration date”, and payments taken for them “shall be refunded to the applicable Guests by Tock”.[^1] My read: a venue selling tickets or deposits months ahead should plan any exit around its forward bookings.

Tock can also end the relationship. It may terminate or suspend “upon notice (which may be furnished via email) and without penalty or liability to Tock” in listed cases, including where a merchant “has not listed or sold any Offerings on Tock for one hundred and twenty (120) days”.[^1]

These are Tock’s published merchant terms. They refer to US regulators such as the Federal Trade Commission, and I did not confirm whether Tock signs Australian venues on them.

## SevenRooms: both ways, notice afterwards

What does SevenRooms’ contract allow? Section 11.8: “Neither party may assign the Agreement without the other party’s prior written consent, except if there is a merger, consolidation or sale of all or substantially all of a party’s stock or assets and provided that the assigning party provides written notice to the other promptly following any such assignment.”[^2]

In plain terms: you hear after the event. The clause is mutual, so a group selling itself can use it too.

Two other clauses travel with the contract to any new owner. Renewal pricing: “The pricing of Fees for any renewal term of the applicable Order Form shall increase by up to 5% of the applicable pricing in the prior term, unless SevenRooms notifies Client of different pricing at least 30 days prior to the commencement of the applicable renewal term.” Renewal itself is automatic “unless either party provides notice of non-renewal at least 90 days prior to commencement of the next Renewal Term”.[^2]

The 5% reads like a cap until the sentence finishes. My read: under any owner, the 90-day window before renewal is when you have leverage, so put it in the calendar.

What happens to your information when the contract ends? Section 6.4: each party will “return or destroy any Confidential Information of the other party in its possession, except for Confidential Information of Client that SevenRooms is required by applicable law to retain or that it has archived on back-up systems”.[^2] The clause does not mention an export. My read: ask for one in writing before the 90-day window closes, not after it.

SevenRooms’ contract also gives some guest data to DoorDash, which now owns SevenRooms. That clause has [its own piece](/writing/sevenrooms-guest-data-doordash/).

## Lightspeed: one way only

Is Lightspeed’s clause mutual? No. You “may not assign any of its rights or obligations under this Agreement without Lightspeed’s prior written consent”, while Lightspeed may assign to a company it merges with, a parent or subsidiary, or a successor in a sale of substantially all its stock or assets.[^3]

After termination, “Lightspeed may immediately deactivate Customer’s account and delete Customer Content”.[^3] The detail is in [Lightspeed can hand your contract to a buyer without asking](/writing/lightspeed-assignment/).

## Olo: the buyer has to sign up

What did Olo’s terms do differently? They make the new owner accept the deal. The Omnivore terms: “Neither party may assign or transfer any part of this Agreement without the prior written consent of the other party except that this Agreement may be assigned without consent: (a) to a person or entity who acquires all or substantially all of the assigning party’s assets, stock or business, and (b) to any affiliate or subsidiary of a party; in each case, so long as the assignee accepts the obligations hereunder in writing.”[^4]

That last condition appears in none of the other four clauses.

These terms have already moved once. They are made with “Olo Inc. (as successor-in-interest to Omnivore Technologies, Inc.”, the business Omnivore once was. On data: “As between Olo and Customer, Customer owns the Customer Data.” Olo may still use it “on an aggregated and anonymized basis for Olo’s business purposes”.[^4]

These are the published terms for Omnivore, Olo’s software for connecting to a point-of-sale system. Olo’s main ordering contracts are not public, and I did not read them.

## Square: silent on its own side

What does Square say? Section 25, in full: “These Terms, and any rights and licences granted to you by these Terms, may not be transferred or assigned by you, and any such attempted transfer or assignment by you will be null and void.”[^5]

I found no other assignment wording in the General Terms. They say what you cannot do and nothing about what Square can. Silence is not a promise either way, so ask.

Square’s terms name its successors once, in the licence you give over your Content, which they define to include photos, logos, products, loyalty programs and promotions: “You grant us and our subsidiaries, affiliates, and successors a worldwide, non-exclusive, royalty-free, fully-paid, transferable, perpetual and sub-licensable right” to use it.[^5] My read: that licence is drafted to outlast a change of owner.

## What a new owner can change without a new contract

Once a buyer holds your contract, how far can it rewrite it? Each of the five reserves the right to change its terms:

| Platform | How the terms can change | Notice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tock | “at any time in our sole discretion”[^1] | None: revised terms “will take effect immediately”[^1] |
| SevenRooms | “SevenRooms may update this Agreement”[^2] | “at least thirty (30) days” for material updates, except those caused by law or “critical service providers”[^2] |
| Lightspeed | Amendments “including making changes to the Fees and scope of the Products”[^3] | “thirty (30) days’ written notice”[^3] |
| Olo, Omnivore | “from time to time at its sole discretion”[^4] | “reasonable notice by email or by posting a notification on the Olo Dashboard” for a significant change[^4] |
| Square | “we may amend the Terms at any time”[^5] | “at least ten (10) days in advance” for a material change[^5] |

Lightspeed lists its reasons, and one is “changes in market conditions”.[^3] SevenRooms can also change its fees on written notice. If an increase goes beyond its renewal rule, you have 30 days to reject it by giving notice of termination. Otherwise you are “deemed to have approved and accepted such changes”.[^2]

Four of the five say that carrying on using the service means accepting the change: SevenRooms, Lightspeed, Olo and Square.[^2][^3][^4][^5] Two spell out a way out. Lightspeed lets you terminate without penalty within the notice period, and Square says “You can stop using the Services at any time by deactivating your Square Account without penalty.”[^3][^5]

My read: after a sale, the notice period is most of your negotiating position. At Tock it is zero.

## What a sale sets in motion

1. A buyer completes the purchase of the vendor, or of the product line.

2. Under four of the five contracts, your agreement can pass to the buyer without your consent. SevenRooms tells you afterwards. Olo’s Omnivore terms make the buyer accept the obligations in writing.

3. The new owner inherits the change clauses in the table above, from immediate changes at Tock to 30 days’ notice at SevenRooms and Lightspeed.

4. Your way out runs through the renewal and termination clauses. Your data runs through the ownership and deletion clauses.

## For your AI lead

My read, in order:

1. Keep a register for every booking, ordering and POS platform: current owner, assignment clause, notice terms, renewal date and the non-renewal window.

2. Hold a monthly export of the guest and booking data each contract says you own. Tock, Olo’s Omnivore terms and SevenRooms (outside its DoorDash clause) all say the customer owns it.

3. Tag every guest record with the system it came from, so a change of owner on one platform does not blur ownership across the rest.

4. List every model, report or integration that reads from a platform’s API. A new owner who changes the product can change that access.

## What this does not show

- Which version of each contract an Australian venue signs. Order forms can change these terms.

- Whether Tock signs Australian venues on the terms quoted here.

- Olo’s main ordering contracts. I read only the published Omnivore terms.

- Whether any of the four sales changed customers’ terms. The releases do not say.

- How a court would read a contract that is silent on the vendor’s right to assign, as Square’s is. This is not legal advice.

The vendors’ written answers to the email below would settle the first and fourth for your own contracts.

## Put this in writing

Send this to each booking, ordering and POS vendor before your next renewal, and keep the replies with the contracts.

> Subject: Assignment, notice and our data on a change of owner
>
> For our agreement with you, please confirm in writing:
>
> 1. Can you assign our agreement without our consent, and in what circumstances?
> 2. How much written notice will we get before, not after, any assignment or change of control?
> 3. Will you give us a full export of our guest and booking data, in a standard format, before any assignment takes effect?
> 4. Will you agree in writing that a new owner must accept our agreement’s terms, including data ownership, for the rest of our term?
